# Israeli Tax Rates 2026

All figures verified against official Israeli Tax Authority publications and Kolzchut.

## Corporate Tax

- Standard rate: **23%**
- Reduced rates for qualifying companies under the Law for Encouragement of Capital Investments:
  - Special Preferred Enterprise in Development Area A: 5%
  - Special Preferred Enterprise elsewhere: 8%
  - Preferred Enterprise in Development Area A: 7.5%
  - Preferred Enterprise elsewhere: 16%
- 2% additional tax on retained corporate profits not distributed as dividends

## Surtax (Mas Yesafim, Section 121B)

- Threshold: **721,560 NIS** annual income (all sources combined)
- Rate: **3%** on all income above threshold
- Additional **2%** on non-labor income (dividends, interest, capital gains, rental) above threshold
- Total surtax on non-labor income above threshold: **5%**

### Controlling Shareholder With Zero Salary (BL Minimum)

A baal shlita drawing no salary still owes Bituach Leumi directly as a non-employee with no taxable employment income. 2026 minimums:

| Component | Monthly Minimum |
|-----------|-----------------|
| NI | ~143 NIS |
| Health | ~123 NIS |
| Combined floor | ~266 NIS |

Liability sits on the individual, not the company. If the shareholder has non-employment income (dividends, rental, interest, capital gains), percentage-based rates apply on that income above the floor.

- Standard rate: **18%** (increased from 17% on January 1, 2025)

## Trapped Profits / Closely-Held Company Reforms (Amendment 277, in force 2025)

Amendment 277 to the Income Tax Ordinance restructured taxation of closely-held companies (chevrot meatim):

- **2% additional corporate-tax surcharge** on excess undistributed retained earnings of a closely-held company. A basic exemption shield (~750,000 NIS) and activity/asset shields apply.
- **Section 62A tightened.** Controlling-shareholder threshold dropped to 25% (was 10% in the old §62A); single-client test reduced to 22 of the prior 36 months (was 30 of 36); profits above a 25% margin on personal-effort revenue are attributed to the shareholder at marginal rates regardless of distribution.
- **+2% surtax on non-labor income** above the 721,560 threshold (in addition to the standard 3% Section 121B surtax). Effective for tax year 2025 onward.

# Section 3(tet), 3(tet1), and 3(yod) -- Deemed Interest Rules

## Legal Basis

Sections 3(tet) and 3(yod) of the Israeli Income Tax Ordinance establish tax liability on loans provided at below-market interest rates between related parties. Section 3(tet1) (Amendment 235, in force 2017) adds an automatic-reclassification rule for shareholder withdrawals that remain outstanding past a hard deadline.

## Section 3(tet) -- Non-CPI-Linked Loans

**2026 prescribed rate:** 6.53%

If a loan or credit is provided without interest or at interest below 6.53%, the difference is deemed taxable income to the borrower. For controlling shareholders, deemed interest is classified as employment income and reported on Form 126.

## Section 3(yod) -- CPI-Linked Loans

**2026 prescribed rate:** 4.9%

For loans between related parties that are CPI-linked, minimum real interest of 4.9% applies.

## Section 3(tet1) -- Automatic Reclassification of Shareholder Withdrawals

Enacted by Amendment 235 (29 Dec 2016, in force 1 Jan 2017). Detailed in Tax Authority Circular 7/2017.

**Rule:** A loan or withdrawal from a closely-held company to a controlling shareholder is automatically deemed withdrawn if not repaid by the **end of the tax year following the year of withdrawal**.

| Scenario | Deemed-withdrawal date |
|----------|----------------------|
| Loan drawn Jan 2026 | Dec 31, 2027 if not repaid |
| Loan drawn Nov 2026 | Dec 31, 2027 if not repaid |
| Loan drawn Jan 2027 | Dec 31, 2028 if not repaid |

**Cascade on deemed withdrawal (per Circular 7/2017):**

1. If the company has distributable retained earnings, the withdrawal is reclassified as a dividend (30% + any applicable surtax for controlling shareholders).
2. If no retained earnings, reclassified as salary (marginal rates + NI + employer NI).
3. Residual cases reclassified as a loan to a related party (subject to Section 3(tet) deemed-interest accrual).

**De minimis exemption:** Cumulative withdrawal balance below NIS 100,000 on every day of year N AND every day of year N-1 is outside §3(tet1).

**Section 3(tet1) also covers "use of asset" (שימוש בנכס):** Amendment 235 explicitly pulls in shareholder personal use of company-owned apartments, vehicles beyond limited business use, art, yachts, and similar assets, valued at deemed annual rental/usage value. This is the single most-missed §3(tet1) trap in real Tax Authority audits. A shareholder living in a company-owned apartment or driving a company-owned car for personal purposes accrues a deemed withdrawal even without a cash loan.

## Deemed Dividend Reclassification Risk

The Tax Authority may reclassify a shareholder loan as a deemed dividend when:

| Risk Factor | Impact |
|-------------|--------|
| No written loan agreement | High risk |
| No repayment schedule | High risk |
| Loan used for personal purposes | Medium-high risk |
| Company has distributable retained earnings | Medium risk |
| Loan balance grows year over year | High risk |
| Loan still outstanding at end of year N+1 | Automatic under §3(tet1) |

**Consequences:** Entire loan taxed as dividend at 30% + potential surtax + interest and penalties.

## Documentation Requirements

1. Written loan agreement signed by both parties
2. Interest rate at least 6.53% (or documented reason for lower)
3. Repayment schedule with specific dates
4. Board resolution approving the loan
5. Proper bookkeeping entries
6. Evidence of actual repayments
7. Year-end review of all shareholder loan balances to track the §3(tet1) deadline

## Recent Section 3(tet) Rate History

| Year | Rate |
|------|------|
| 2026 | 6.53% |
| 2025 | 5.83% |

The annual rate is set by regulation in December for the following tax year. Practitioners should confirm the current-year rate against the Tax Authority's annual circular before applying historical figures.

# Profit Extraction Methods: Detailed Comparison

Worked examples for each extraction method from an Israeli limited company (Chevra Baam).

## Assumptions for All Examples

- Company: Israeli Ltd (Chevra Baam)
- Shareholder: Single controlling shareholder (100% ownership, baal shlita)
- Tax year: 2026
- Shareholder has 2.25 base credit points (resident male)
- No other income sources (for simplicity)

## Example 1: Small Extraction (200,000 NIS Company Profit)

### Method A: Full Salary

Gross salary approximately 186,270 NIS/year (after deducting employer NI from budget)
- Income tax (progressive): approximately 24,690 NIS
- Credit points reduction: -6,534 NIS
- Net income tax: approximately 18,156 NIS
- Employee NI+health: approximately 12,920 NIS
- Net to shareholder: approximately 155,194 NIS
- **Effective rate: approximately 22.4%**

### Method B: Full Dividend

- Corporate tax (23%): 46,000 NIS
- Distributable: 154,000 NIS
- Dividend tax (30%): 46,200 NIS
- Net to shareholder: 107,800 NIS
- **Effective rate: 46.1%**

### Result: Salary wins by ~47,394 NIS

For amounts within the lower tax brackets, salary is significantly better.

## Example 2: Medium Extraction (500,000 NIS Company Profit)

### Optimal Mix (Salary 228,000 + Dividend on Rest)

Salary portion: 228,000 gross (employer cost approximately 244,837)
- Employee tax: approximately 25,765 NIS (after credit points)
- Employee NI: approximately 18,960 NIS
- Net from salary: approximately 183,275 NIS

Remaining profit: approximately 255,163 NIS
- Corporate tax: 58,687 NIS
- Distributable: 196,476 NIS
- Dividend tax: 58,943 NIS
- Net from dividend: approximately 137,533 NIS

Total net: approximately 320,808 NIS
**Effective rate: approximately 35.8%**

Compared to pure dividend (46.1%) this saves approximately 48,767 NIS.

## Key Decision Thresholds

| Annual Extraction Amount | Recommended Strategy |
|-------------------------|---------------------|
| Under 84,120 NIS | Pure salary (10% bracket, with credit points near 0%) |
| 84,120 - 228,000 NIS | Pure salary (up to 20% bracket, well below 46.1%) |
| 228,000 - 560,280 NIS | Salary at 228,000, dividend for rest |
| Above 560,280 NIS | Salary at 228,000, dividend for rest, surtax planning |
